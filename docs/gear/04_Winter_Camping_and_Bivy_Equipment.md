# 4. Winter Camping, Snow Shelters & Bivy Equipment

Multi-day ski traverses (e.g., Haute Route, Waddington Range, Denali, Bugaboos) require self-sufficient basecamp or alpine bivouac logistics. In a game, camping governs **Overnight Stamina Recovery, Hydration (Melting Snow), and Storm Survival**.

## A. Shelters
1. **Single-Wall Breathable Alpine Assault Tent (1.2kg–1.8kg):**
   * *Benchmarks:* Samaya 2.0 / 2.5 (Dyneema composite floor + electrospun waterproof-breathable membrane walls), Black Diamond Firstlight / Eldorado, Rab Latok Mountain.
   * *Visuals:* Tiny footprint designed to pitch on narrow chopped snow ledges on mountain ridges; internal or external DAC Featherlite poles; bright high-visibility yellow, orange, or pink fabric.
2. **Double-Wall 4-Season Geodesic Expedition Tent (2.8kg–4.2kg):**
   * *Benchmarks:* Hilleberg Jannu / Nammatj / Tarra, The North Face VE 25 / Assault, Mountain Hardwear Trango 3,MSR Access / Remote.
   * *Visuals:* Intersecting multi-pole geodesic dome or tunnel geometry capable of shedding 100+ km/h gale-force winds and heavy overnight snow loads; perimeter **snow flaps/valances** (buried with snow to seal out spindrift); dual vestibules for digging a cold-sink foot trench and cooking safely.
3. **Ultralight Floorless Pyramid Mid (0.5kg–0.8kg):**
   * *Benchmarks:* Hyperlite Mountain Gear Ultamid 2/4 (Dyneema DCF), Black Diamond Beta Light, Locus Gear Khufu.
   * *Visuals:* Pitched using the player's own ski poles strapped together at the center apex, anchored with skis and ice axes as deadmen; players dig down into the snowpack underneath the tarp to create bench seating and a standing kitchen pit.
4. **Emergency Bivy Sack & Bothy Bag (150g–400g):**
   * *Benchmarks:* Outdoor Research Helium Bivy, Rab Storm Bivy, Terra Nova Bothy 2/4.
   * *Function:* Emergency survival shelter for unplanned open bivouacs or storm sit-outs; keeps wind off the sleeping bag at the cost of higher condensation.

## B. Sleep System (Insulation from the Snow)
Conductive heat loss into the snowpack will drain player body heat faster than air temperature.

1. **Dual-Pad System (Combined R-Value 6.5–8.5+):**
   * *Closed-Cell Foam Pad (R-2.0):* Therm-a-Rest Z Lite Sol or Switchback (accordion-style dimpled foam with reflective aluminized surface). Strapped to the outside of the pack; used as a sit pad during lunch breaks and placed *under* the inflatable pad at night to prevent punctures on rock/ice.
   * *Insulated Winter Air Pad (R-6.0 to R-8.5):* Therm-a-Rest NeoAir XTherm NXT (triangular core matrix with reflective ThermaCapture baffles), Exped Ultra 7R / Dura 8R (down-filled air tubes), Nemo Tensor Extreme Conditions.
2. **Expedition Down Sleeping Bag (-18°C / 0°F to -40°C / -40°F):**
   * *Benchmarks:* Western Mountaineering Puma GWS / Antelope MF, Feathered Friends Snowbunting / Ibis, Rab Neutrino Pro 900, Mountain Hardwear Phantom.
   * *Visuals:* Mummy cut with thick 3D draft collar around the neck, full-length draft tube along zipper, waterproof Pertex Quantum Pro shell at the head and footbox (where the bag touches frost/condensation on tent walls).
3. **Vapor Barrier Liner (VBL) & Down Booties:**
   * *Down Camp Booties:* Feathered Friends Down Booties (removable waterproof outer shell over goose down inner sock for walking around camp).
   * *VBL:* Non-breathable silnylon liner worn inside the sleeping bag on multi-week arctic expeditions to prevent body perspiration from freezing inside the down feathers over consecutive nights.

## C. Winter Kitchen, Hydration & Snow Melting
In winter above freezing line, liquid water doesn't exist—every liter of water must be melted from snow using fuel.

1. **Stoves:**
   * *Inverted Liquid Fuel / White Gas Expedition Stove:* MSR XGK EX (roarer burner that sounds like a jet engine; melts 1L of snow in ~3 minutes; field-maintainable with a shaker jet needle), MSR WhisperLite International, Optimus Polaris Optifuel.
   * *Pressure-Regulated Reactor Canister Stove:* MSR Reactor 1.7L / 2.5L (radiant catalytic burner enclosed in a windproof heat-exchanger pot; unbeaten fuel efficiency in high winds, glows bright infrared orange).
   * *Insulated Stove Base:* Wooden or composite board (e.g., Trillium base) placed under the stove so it doesn't melt a hole straight down into the snow floor.
2. **Fuel Consumption Mechanics:**
   * Melting snow at -15°C requires roughly **30g–45g of fuel per liter** of boiling water (accounting for latent heat of fusion).
   * Players must carry enough white gas (red aluminum MSR fuel bottles) or isobutane canisters to produce 3L–4L of water per day per person, or suffer `Dehydration` (which increases frostbite risk and reduces max stamina by 30%).
3. **Hydration Storage (Anti-Freeze):**
   * *Wide-Mouth HDPE Nalgene (1L) in Insulated Neoprene Sleeve:* Stored upside-down in the pack (because water freezes from the top down; if stored upside down, the ice forms at the bottom and the cap still opens). At night, filled with boiling water and placed inside the sleeping bag as a radiant heater.
   * *Vacuum Insulated Titanium/Steel Thermos (0.5L–0.75L):* Hydro Flask Trail Lightweight or Zojirushi for instant hot tea on windy summits.
   * *Note:* Hydration bladders/tubes are generally avoided in extreme cold because the exposed drinking tube freezes solid within minutes.
4. **Camp Construction Tools:**
   * *Snow Saw (G3 Bonesaw / Black Diamond Snow Saw Pro):* 35cm stainless steel serrated blade that attaches to ski poles; used for cutting snow blocks to build wind walls around tents, igloos, or snow caves, as well as cutting ECT (Extended Column Test) snowpack stability pits.
