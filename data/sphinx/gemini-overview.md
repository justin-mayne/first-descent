**The Sphinx** (Southeast Ridge/Face) is an iconic, diamond-shaped ski mountaineering line in the Chugach Mountains outside Cordova, Alaska. Featured in Chris Davenport, Art Burrows, and Penn Newhard's guidebook *Fifty Classic Ski Descents of North America*, it served as **Line 38 of 50** in professional skier Cody Townsend's multi-year project, *The FIFTY*. Because Townsend's project required climbing every line from the bottom up rather than taking a standard helicopter drop at the summit, The Sphinx became one of his most mentally taxing objectives—requiring an initial bail in 2019 before a successful redemption ascent and descent in April 2022.

## Detailed Text-Based Route Description

### 1. The Approach & Glacier Apron

Accessing the base of The Sphinx requires either a multi-day, human-powered expedition across remote Alaskan glaciers or a helicopter drop onto the glacier floor via Points North Heli-Adventures (which Townsend used to reach the base of the climb). From the landing zone on the glacier flats, the route ascends a broad, low-angle glacial apron toward the towering, planar Southeast Face. Even on the apron, route-finding requires careful glacier navigation to avoid hidden crevasses and overhead exposure from warming cornices or sluff shedding off the main face.

### 2. The Bergschrund Crossing

At the top of the lower apron, the glacier pulls away from the steep mountain wall, creating a massive, gaping **bergschrund** that spans the entire width of the base.

* **On the Ascent:** Climbers must locate a viable snow bridge—typically pinched toward the looker's left or right edge of the exit choke—to transition from the 35-degree apron onto the 50-degree main face.
* **On the Descent:** The bergschrund acts as a high-consequence terrain trap at the bottom of the funnel. Everything sliding down the upper face channels directly over it.

### 3. The Main Face & Ascent Track

Above the bergschrund, the mountain rears back into an unrelenting, diamond-shaped planar face of **sustained 50°+ terrain**.

* **Bootpacking the Face:** Unlike standard heli-skiers who are dropped directly onto the summit pin, Townsend and his partners had to transition to boot crampons, two ice axes (or whippets/axes), and bootpack straight up the 1,800-foot face.
* **Double Fall-Line Geometry:** The face is not a flat ramp; it features a subtle **double fall-line** that tilts both downward toward the glacier and slightly laterally toward the cliff-lined edges and central exit gully. On foot, this creates strict "fall-you-die" exposure for over an hour of vertical climbing, where self-arrest on firm snow or over shallow rocks is nearly impossible.
* **The Summit Ridge:** Near the top, the face narrows toward a sharp, corniced ridge and needle-like summit. Climbers must navigate steep spine features and massive Alaskan cornices to top out on a tiny, exposed perch at 6,299 feet.

### 4. The Ski Descent

Dropping in from the true summit of The Sphinx is one of the most intimidating entrances in North American big-mountain skiing:

* **The Blind Rollover:** From the narrow summit perch, the initial entry is a steep, **blind rollover** where the skier cannot see the middle of the face until committing over the convex horizon line.
* **The Upper Diamond & Ramp:** Once over the rollover, the line opens onto the main diamond face—a 50-to-55-degree, double fall-line ramp. Because the entire upper face funnels directly into a narrower gully/choke just above the bergschrund, **sluff management** is the primary survival challenge. As Townsend noted in his route beta, you either have to ski it at mach-speed like an elite freerider to outrun your sluff in one continuous top-to-bottom burn, or ski it tactically in pitches—cutting across the fall line to safe ribs/edges and letting the fast-moving powder cascades thunder past before continuing.
* **The Funnel & Exit Air:** The lower third of the face pinches into an exit chute right above the bergschrund. Skiers must ensure all sluff from their upper turns has cleared this bottleneck before dropping through the choke, airing or threading across the bergschrund snow bridge, and running out onto the mellow glacier apron below.

## Cody Townsend’s Two Attempts on The Sphinx

### Attempt 1: April 2019 — "Line */50" (The Bail)

In the inaugural season of *The FIFTY*, Townsend and cinematographer Bjarne Salén received a last-minute call that weather had cleared in Cordova and rushed north.

* **What Happened:** After being dropped at the base, Townsend and Salén crossed the bergschrund and began bootpacking up the Southeast Face. Partway up the 50-degree wall, they encountered wind-pressed, variable, and unforgiving hardpack/crust over shallow sharks (buried rocks) rather than deep, hero Alaskan powder.
* **The Decision:** Facing extreme exposure where a single lost edge or boot slip would result in an unstoppable, fatal slide over the bergschrund, mounting fear and deteriorating snow stability forced them to call it. They transitioned mid-face, carefully skied down the lower portion in survival mode, and left the line unfinished as "Line */50".

### Attempt 2: April 2022 — "Line 38/50" (The Redemption)

After three years of monitoring Cordova weather and snowpack reports from afar, a prime window opened in April 2022 immediately after Townsend finished Line 37.

* **The Crew:** Townsend teamed up with professional freeride snowboarder **Nick Russell**, guide **Ed Shanley**, and filmmaker **Bjarne Salén**.
* **What Happened:** Returning to Points North Heli-Adventures, the crew found The Sphinx plastered in stable, boot-top-to-knee-deep velvet powder. With four years of hardened ski mountaineering experience under his belt, Townsend, Russell, and Shanley crossed the bergschrund and punched a relentless bootpack all the way to the 6,299-foot summit pin.
* **The Ride & Gear:** Dropping in one by one over the blind summit rollover, the crew linked high-speed, big-mountain turns down the 50-degree ramp, managing heavy sluff walls before airing across the bergschrund onto the apron. For the descent, Townsend skied on 112mm-underfoot **Salomon QST Blank** skis mounted with **Salomon MTN** tech bindings, carrying a **Mammut RAS** avalanche airbag pack.
